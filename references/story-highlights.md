# Scene-Supported Story Highlights

Choose the highlight after accepting the curated base and before the final style edit.
The goal is atmosphere and story, not decoration.

## Selection Rule

Choose exactly one primary highlight. Add one secondary cue only when it belongs to the
same quiet story and remains visibly subordinate. Prefer an existing scene element;
when needed, add only a small atmospheric cue directly implied by a visible object.

Strong candidates include:

- a lamp with a restrained warm pool of graphic light;
- daylight or a colored reflection shaped by a visible window or opening;
- a kettle, cup, pot, or bath with a small amount of steam;
- a plant or curtain carrying a faint suggestion of air movement;
- an open book, unfinished drink, sleeping pet, textile, or personal display that
  makes the room feel recently used;
- one existing small object strengthened as the saturated color accent.

Choose the candidate that best reinforces the room's actual function and lived-in
character. For sparse scenes, a tiny atmospheric cue is enough. For already expressive
scenes, strengthen an existing feature rather than adding another one.

## Boundaries

- The highlight may be artistically interpreted; it need not reproduce photographic
  lighting or every source detail.
- Keep it locally plausible and supported by a visible object, opening, activity, or
  functional zone.
- Do not add large furniture, people, fantasy events, theatrical weather, dramatic
  beams, floating objects, or unrelated decorative props.
- Do not create several competing focal points or let the highlight obscure spatial
  reading.
- Preserve the established color relationships and white-line system. Use a small
  accent or flat tonal plane rather than cinematic gradients and realistic glow.

Accept the highlight when it makes the viewer imagine a moment in the room while the
space itself still reads first.
