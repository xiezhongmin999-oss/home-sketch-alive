# Fixed Style: Abstract Graphic Interior Illustration

Apply this single style after accepting the lightly curated base. Do not offer, select,
blend, or substitute other styles.

Before writing the style prompt, read [story-highlights.md](story-highlights.md) and
choose the scene-supported highlight.

The accepted curated base is the sole authority for observation method, camera,
projection, perspective, vanishing points, composition, crop, spatial proportions,
architecture, openings, zones, major furniture, and lived-in details. Change the
visual expression, not the corrected space.

## Visual Character

- Aim for 90% graphic illustration and 10% hand-drawn texture.
- Create a flat architectural editorial interior illustration with restrained
  cut-paper collage and gouache character.
- Make it feel like a highly distilled architectural or lifestyle magazine image:
  concise, relaxed, lived-in, spatially clear, and design-led.
- Let bold color blocks and spatial organization read first. Let white hand-drawn
  lines and subtle paper texture appear on closer inspection.
- Avoid traditional architectural watercolor and photographic or 3D-render realism.

## Shape Language

- Convert visible furniture and objects into recognizable geometric symbols and
  interlocking matte color shapes.
- Preserve real proportions, orientation, placement, and object categories while
  removing mechanical detail and complex small structures.
- Express retained small-object groups as a few representative silhouettes, color
  blocks, or geometric marks instead of reproducing every item.
- Do not add nonexistent large furniture or redesign the room for a better picture.

## Warm-White Line System

- Outline walls, openings, major furniture, and selected identity objects with white
  or warm-white lines only. Never use black contours.
- Use moderately bold lines with slight wobble, retracing, offset, soft corners,
  incomplete joins, and occasional open contours.
- Keep the linework loose and architect-like, with a quick, relaxed sketch quality,
  while maintaining clear structural reading.
- Avoid CAD precision, perfect vector geometry, and uniform outlines around every
  tiny object.

## Color

- Use about 8–10 principal colors derived from the source.
- Favor warm beige, cream, sand, warm brown, greige, sage, deep olive, blue-gray,
  dark blue-gray, and terracotta.
- Preserve broad source color identities: green furniture stays in the green family,
  dark textiles stay dark blue-gray or charcoal, and wood stays warm brown or ochre.
- Allow only one or two small saturated accents, such as orange, cobalt blue, purple,
  bright yellow, or brick red. Limit them to a chair, cushion, book, lamp, small item,
  or decoration; never spread accent colors across the whole image.

## Material and Paper

- Remove nearly all photographic material realism: no realistic wood grain, fabric
  folds, tile reflections, plastic, metal, glass, gloss, or complex highlights.
- Describe surfaces with broad flat color, a few simple geometric marks, and restrained
  paper, gouache, screen-print, crayon, or handmade-collage texture.
- Use a warm off-white, pale kraft, or warm greige paper ground with very subtle fibers,
  grain, and print texture. Keep it clean, not dirty, distressed, heavily yellowed, or
  nostalgic-poster-like.

## Depth and Light

- Preserve the source observation method exactly. Where the source contains depth,
  express it through overlap, front-to-back hierarchy, wall thickness, object massing,
  and minimal flat contact shadows.
- Do not use realistic lighting to manufacture depth. Avoid cinematic light, strong
  ambient occlusion, photographic gradients, complex highlights, and deep shadows.
- For orthographic, plan, elevation, or other non-perspective sources, do not introduce
  perspective or extra 3D effects.
- A selected light-based highlight may use one restrained flat glow plane or tonal
  shift. Keep it graphic and local rather than cinematic or photorealistic.

## Style Edit Prompt

Use this complete block together with the scene-specific locks from the source brief:

    Transform this lightly curated interior into a hand-drawn abstract graphic
    architectural space illustration. Change only the visual expression. The accepted
    curated base is the sole authority for camera, observation direction, projection,
    perspective, vanishing points, composition, crop, room proportions, architecture,
    openings, zones, major furniture position, orientation, relative size, function,
    and retained lived-in details. Preserve it exactly. Do not recrop, re-correct the
    view, or redesign the scene.

    Establish this scene-supported storytelling highlight: [PRIMARY HIGHLIGHT]. If it
    strengthens the same quiet story without competing, add this subordinate cue:
    [SECONDARY CUE OR NONE]. The highlight may gently enhance an existing light,
    steam, breeze, reflection, open book, cup, pet, textile, plant, or small personal
    display, or add only a tiny plausible atmospheric cue implied by an existing
    object. Do not add unrelated decor or large objects.

    Use 90% flat editorial graphic design and 10% hand-drawn texture. Build the visible
    room and objects from simplified interlocking matte color blocks with restrained
    cut-paper collage and gouache character. Use about 8–10 source-derived colors and
    only one or two small saturated accents. Preserve broad source color identities.
    Outline walls, openings, major furniture, and selected identity objects with
    moderately bold, slightly irregular white or warm-white hand-drawn lines; use no
    black contours. Remove photographic material realism. Express surfaces with flat
    color, minimal geometric marks, and subtle clean paper grain. Show retained small
    objects as readable symbols. Express existing depth only through overlap, plane
    hierarchy, wall thickness, object massing, and minimal flat contact shadows, never
    through realistic rendering or invented perspective. Do not restore removed
    clutter, clear all signs of life, add people or text, or add nonexistent large
    furniture.

## Reject If

Reject and retry if any of these occur:

- view, camera, projection, perspective, vanishing points, composition, or crop drift;
- room proportions, walls, openings, zones, or major furniture change;
- a non-perspective source gains perspective or one source type becomes another;
- cleaned clutter returns or the home is emptied of lived-in evidence;
- black outlines, CAD linework, uniform vector precision, or excessive micro-detail;
- photorealistic materials, reflections, lighting, gradients, ambient occlusion, or
  deep shadows;
- traditional architectural watercolor, 3D-model screenshot, interior-render,
  miniature-dollhouse, photographic, dirty-paper, or retro-poster appearance.
- an unrelated, oversized, theatrical, or composition-dominating highlight.
